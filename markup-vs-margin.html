Markup vs margin for contractors

The single most expensive arithmetic mistake in the trades, with the conversion table both ways.

Markup and margin are not the same number. Markup is a percentage of your cost; margin is a percentage of your price. Adding 18% to your cost earns you a 15.3% margin, not 18%. To actually earn an 18% margin you must add a 22.0% markup.

The two formulas

margin = markup ÷ (1 + markup)
markup = margin ÷ (1 − margin)
price = cost ÷ (1 − margin)

The third formula is the one to use. Decide what share of the price you intend to keep, then divide cost by one minus that share. It gives you the margin you asked for every time, regardless of how the cost is made up.

A worked example

A bathroom remodel with a total cost of $5,672.54, including materials loaded for waste and tax, labor loaded for burden, and overhead recovered per labor hour.

MethodPriceMargin earned
Cost × 1.18 (the shortcut)$6,693.6015.3%
Cost ÷ 0.82 (correct)$6,917.7318.0%

The difference is $224.13 on one job. A contractor running twenty jobs a year leaves $4,482.69 on the table without ever seeing it, because both prices look like they contain 18%.

The rule of thumb that causes the damage: "add 20% and you're making 20%." At a 20% markup you are making 16.7%. At a 50% markup you are making 33.3%. The gap widens as the number grows.

Why it survives in the trades

Because both numbers are right, just about different denominators, and nothing about a quote reveals which one you used. A job priced on markup still comes in, still gets paid, and still looks profitable on the invoice. The shortfall only appears at the end of the year, in a profit figure that is consistently a few points below the one you planned.

Frequently asked questions

Is markup the same as margin?

No. Markup is measured against your cost. Margin is measured against your price. A 50% markup produces a 33.3% margin, not a 50% margin.

What markup do I need for a 20% margin?

25%. The formula is markup = margin / (1 - margin), so 0.20 / 0.80 = 0.25.

What margin does a 20% markup give me?

16.7%. The formula is margin = markup / (1 + markup), so 0.20 / 1.20 = 0.167.

Why does this matter on a small job?

Because it compounds. On a $5,672 cost the gap between an 18% markup and an 18% margin is $224. Across 20 jobs a year that is $4,483 of profit that was never priced in.

Which should contractors price from?

Margin. Decide the share of the price you intend to keep, then divide your cost by (1 - margin). Pricing from markup means your actual margin changes every time your cost mix changes.
