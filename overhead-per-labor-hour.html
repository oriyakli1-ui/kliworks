How to calculate overhead per labor hour

Why overhead is not a percentage of the job, and the two-number formula that replaces the guess.

Overhead per labor hour = annual overhead ÷ annual billable labor hours. $85,000 of overhead spread across 3,400 billable hours is $25.00 per hour, and every hour you bill must carry it before you earn a cent of profit.

The formula

overhead rate = annual overhead ÷ annual billable labor hours
$85,000 ÷ 3,400 = $25.00 per billable hour

Why a percentage is the wrong instrument

Adding "10% for overhead" ties your office rent to the price of cabinets. It is the wrong driver. Your truck, your insurance, your phone and your admin time are consumed by time on the job, not by the dollar value of what you install.

Two jobs, each two days of labor. One has $8,000 of cabinets, the other $800 of trim. They consume identical overhead. A percentage charges the first ten times as much as the second and misprices both.

The two numbers, carefully

Annual overhead

Everything that exists whether or not you are on a job: rent, insurance, vehicle payments and fuel, phone, software, advertising, licensing, accounting, and any admin or office wages. Field labor, job material and subcontractors are direct costs and do not belong here.

Annual billable hours

Hours you can actually invoice. This is the number most contractors get wrong, because it is far below hours worked. Drive time, quoting, chasing suppliers, warranty callbacks and shop time are all real work and none of it is billable. A two-person crew that each works 2,000 hours may only bill 3,400 between them.

Dividing by hours worked instead of hours billed understates your overhead rate — which means you under-recover on every job, all year, and never see where it went.

What to do with the number

Multiply it by the labor hours in the job. An estimate carrying 45.59 labor hours absorbs 45.59 × $25.00 = $1,139.75 of overhead. Add that to your direct cost before you apply any margin, and you have a cost that is actually complete.

Price from that figure using margin rather than markup and the number you quote finally contains everything it is supposed to.

Frequently asked questions

How do I calculate overhead per labor hour?

Divide your annual overhead by your annual billable labor hours. $85,000 of overhead across 3,400 billable hours is $25.00 per hour.

What counts as overhead?

Costs that exist whether or not you are on a job: rent, insurance, vehicle payments and fuel, phone, software, advertising, licensing, and any admin or office pay. Job-specific material, field labor and subcontractors are direct costs, not overhead.

Should I use worked hours or billable hours?

Billable hours. Drive time, quoting, warranty callbacks and shop time are real but not billable, and dividing by hours you cannot invoice understates your rate.

Why not just add a percentage for overhead?

Because overhead does not scale with material cost. A job with $8,000 of cabinets and two days of labor carries the same two days of overhead as a job with $800 of trim and two days of labor. Charging a percentage overcharges material-heavy jobs and undercharges labor-heavy ones.

What is a typical overhead recovery rate?

It varies with the size of the operation, but $20 to $40 per billable hour is a common range for a small contractor. The figure only means something when it is calculated from your own numbers.
