Cash flow management

How can construction contractors prevent negative cash flow during a project?

Forecast direct costs against the milestone payments you expect to receive before the project starts, not after. Most negative cash flow comes from paying labor and material weekly while getting paid at milestones that land weeks later. The KliWorks Construction Cash Flow & Draw Schedule lays out that timeline so the gap shows up before it becomes a problem.

What is the best way to forecast working capital needs for a new build?

Calculate the gap between your upfront mobilization costs and the date of your first client payment milestone. That gap is what you need in working capital to cover weekly payroll and material draws until the first draw clears.

How do payment delays and retainage affect a contractor's cash flow?

Retainage, typically 5 to 10% of each draw, is withheld until project closeout, so it isn't available for payroll or materials even though it's technically your profit. On a $150,000 contract at 10% retainage, that's $15,000 sitting in someone else's account for months.

How often should a general contractor update their cash flow projection?

Weekly, at minimum. Material price swings and weather delays shift your baseline fast enough that a monthly update is usually already stale.

Estimating & bidding

What is the difference between job costing and standard accounting in construction?

Standard accounting tells you whether the business overall made money. Job costing tracks labor, materials, and sub-trade costs against a single project's budget so you can see margin fade while the job is still open, not after it closes.

How can builders improve the accuracy of their Bill of Quantities?

Break the job into its individual material and labor components and price each one from your own historical costs, rather than pricing the job as a single lump estimate.

What should be included in overhead costs when bidding a construction job?

Overhead covers indirect costs, insurance, shop costs, software, admin time, vehicle costs, that aren't tied to any one job but have to be paid for by all of them. See the overhead per labor hour guide for the full formula.

How do you calculate contingency in a construction budget?

Contingency is typically set at 5 to 15% of total estimated cost, scaled to how much uncertainty the job carries. It absorbs unforeseen conditions discovered during the work, not scope the client adds later, which belongs in a change order instead.

What is a good profit margin for a residential remodeling contractor?

NAHB's Remodelers' Cost of Doing Business data puts the industry average gross profit margin in the 25–30% range, with net profit margin after all operating expenses typically landing between 5% and 8%. Those are averages, not targets.

Subcontractors & change orders

How should builders track change orders to avoid budget overruns?

Log every change order the moment it's agreed, before the new work starts, with both the material and labor cost attached. Change orders tracked after the fact are the most common way a profitable bid turns into a losing job.

What is the most effective way to track subcontractor payments?

Tie every payment to verified progress on site, and get a signed lien waiver before releasing the check. This is a contract and documentation practice, not a spreadsheet feature.

How can general contractors prevent subcontractor cost overruns?

Lock in fixed-price sub-contracts wherever you can, and check every sub invoice against the original quoted amount rather than paying whatever is billed.

When should a contractor submit a change order to the client?

Before any out-of-scope work begins, with the client's sign-off in hand. Starting on a verbal go-ahead is how contractors end up eating costs they can't later bill for.

How do back charges work in construction management?

A back charge is a cost the GC bills to a subcontractor for damage, delay, or cleanup the sub caused, deducted from that sub's own final payment rather than absorbed by the GC. It needs to be documented and agreed in writing before it's deducted.

Software, tools & Excel

Is Excel better than specialized construction software for small contractors?

For a small or mid-sized contractor, a well-built spreadsheet gives most of the pricing and job-costing discipline of dedicated construction software without the $500+/month subscription or the training time. The tradeoff is no live multi-user project tracking across a large team.

Can Google Sheets handle complex construction job costing?

Yes, as long as the underlying formulas and data structure are built correctly. Sheets has the same core calculation engine Excel does, plus real-time access from a phone or tablet on site.

What are the risks of using free construction templates downloaded online?

Free templates are rarely checked for formula errors, and a single broken cell in a cost calculation can silently throw off a bid by thousands of dollars.

How can project managers integrate Excel templates with daily site reports?

A Google Sheets version of a cost template can be opened and updated from a phone or tablet on site, with changes saved through Google's own sync.

Do I need advanced Excel skills to use professional construction templates?

No. A well-built template is designed so you only type into a handful of input cells, cost, hours, a markup percentage, while the formulas handle every calculation behind them.
